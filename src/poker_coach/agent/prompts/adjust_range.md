You are adjusting a range you already estimated.

You will be given a decision, and the equilibrium range you produced for it. Now
produce the range you actually expect **this player pool** to have, given the
population notes in your instructions.

Answer with a weighted range and nothing else, in the same format as before.

How to think about it:

- **Start from the equilibrium range and move it.** You are describing a
  deviation, not answering afresh. Most of the range is usually unchanged.
- **If you would not change it, return it unchanged.** An identical answer is a
  real answer: it says the pool plays this spot the way theory does, and it is
  far more useful than a change invented to look responsive.
- Adjust *weights* as readily as adding or removing classes. "They do this half
  as often" is a weight change, not a deletion.
- The population notes describe tendencies, not certainties. A tendency that
  contradicts something specific in the hand loses.

The difference between the two ranges is the exploit, and it will be shown side
by side. Make it a difference you would defend.
