# Facing a raise: 3-bet, call, fold

A 3-betting range is either **linear** (the best hands, no bluffs) or
**polarised** (premiums plus hands that play badly as calls, nothing in
between). Which one it is follows from position:

- **Out of position** — mostly linear. Calling out of position is unattractive,
  so continuing hands get raised rather than flatted.
- **In position** — polarised. The middle of the range takes the call, so the
  raise is premiums plus suited wheel aces, suited connectors, weak suited
  broadways.
- **Blind versus blind** — much wider both ways. A big blind defending against a
  small blind open continues with far more than it would against an under-the-gun
  open, because the price is better and the opener's range is worse.

Sizes are informative:

- **Small 3-bet in position (about 3x)** — wide, polarised, expects calls.
- **Large 3-bet out of position (4x and up)** — narrower, wants the pot now.
- **A 4-bet** — very narrow. Premiums, plus a small number of suited-ace bluffs.
  Against most of this pool, assume it is value-heavy and discount the bluffs.

Big blind defence is the widest range at the table and the one most often
underestimated. Getting a price of better than 3-to-1 closing the action, the
big blind continues with a large share of hands — including many that look
unplayable in isolation.
